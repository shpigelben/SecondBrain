Energy transfer, especially in the context of thermodynamics, occurs in one of three ways - heat transfer, thermodynamic work and transfer of mass

## Heat Transfer

Heat transfer $Q$ refers to the transition of momentum between components (atoms, molecules dividers etc) of a thermodynamic system.

**it is the transfer of energy (averaged) on the microscopic level**

In an isolated system, where mass is conserved and there is no external work being done on or by the system, the change in internal energy of the system is given only by means of heat transfer

$$ \Delta U = Q $$

or in a differential form 

$$ dU = \delta Q $$
___

## Thermodynamic Work

Thermodynamic work can be mechanical work done on or by the boundary of the container, it can also be electric or magnetic work. In the absence of heat and mass transfer the change in internal energy of a system is solely due to work done on or by the system

**it is the transfer of energy (averaged) on the macroscopic level**

$$ \Delta U = \pm W $$

or in differential form 

$$ d U = \pm \delta W $$

Where it is $+W$ when the work is done **on** the system thereby providing it with more internal energy,  and $-W$ when the work is done **by** the system on the expanse of its internal energy

#### mechanical work

$$W=-P\Delta V$$

- if $\Delta V < 0$ - the volume contracts and the work on the system is positive, thereby increasing its internal energy
- if $\Delta V > 0$ - the volume expands and the work on the system is negative, or in other words the system does work on its surrounding thereby decreasing its internal energy

### The first Law of Thermodynamics
Both forms of energy transfer stated above constitute the [[the Laws of Thermodynamics | first law of thermodynamics]] for isolated systems (where transfer of mass is not possible), given by 

$$ \Delta U = Q \pm W \ \longmapsto \ dU = \delta Q \pm \delta W $$
