The Zeroth law states that at zero kelvin there is zero entropy. Even though this law excludes certain materials (like water which has entropy greater than zero due to quantum effects at absolute zero).

>A system can arrive at a state where thermodynamics is the right description.

Two separate systems with equal temperature (in which heat exchange no longer occurs) are said to be in **mutual thermodynamic equilibrium**. 
The system that constitutes the two separate systems is in **internal thermodynamic equilibrium** as a whole.

>If A & B are in mutual thermodynamic equilibrium, and A & C are in mutual thermodynamic equilibrium then so are B&C
