I remember from the start that I liked it less than I did [[Startdust]]. In fact, where Startdust’s writing felt a little bit more lyrical and poetic than the standard, this book felt straight away like something out of Stephen King’s collection. As I kept reading the parallels in writing style to the Horror king became more apparent.

The protagonist of the book is a 7 year old boy. I can’t recall my internal monologues from this era of my life or the way in which I thought about the world, but I found Gaiman’s writing of the boy’s internal narrative at times convincing and others not so much.

In practice the story is a recollection of the boy’s older version of past event. Here, in contrast to stardust, the fantastical elements bleed into the real world and the separation doesn’t clearly exist. It is much more a magical realism book in that regard. It fits well a possible interpretation of the book which is a retelling of painful childhood events through the lens of storytelling, a sort of coping mechanism with tough reality that fits the boy’s love towards reading books.
