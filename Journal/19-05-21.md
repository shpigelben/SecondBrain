It's about time I started writing seriously about the experiences in my post-army trip.

A trip to the southeast asian region. Right now Im in India, the largest democracy in the world and the largest subcontinent in the far east. India is also the country with the harshest poverty inhabitting an overwhelming precentage of unimployed civilians. India is by no means a comfortable or an easy place to start touring the southeast region. While alot of places are suited for tourist with travel agencies, guesthouses and resturants most of India is, naturaly, very foreign to the western traveler in almost every aspect of it. Views of poor and sick people in the streets begging for aid will make your breakfast dilemmas seem ridiculous and will not leave you feeling good about yourself when shopping in the bazaars. The notorious Indian food is known for it's unpleasant effects on our fragile digestive system and takes time to adapt to (both mentally and physically). As deterrent as that might be, thousands of travellers choose to tour India regardless of these drawbacks. The variety of landscapes, the cheap prices that provide the ability to travel for long periods the different culture and much more attract tourists. For me India is (boldly) the first station in my 7 month trip. I'm a 22 years old ex-soldier and so my writing this will probably be more suited to the younger audience who seek the thrill of travel before entering the main stage of their lives. Also, English is not my native language, so I apologize in advance if the writing and grammar may be lacking.

  

Mombai - From Israel (my home country) me and two of my companions landed in Mombai and stayed there for one night before departuring to Delhi. Mombai as my first city in india was alot to take in, and as an unexperienced traveler that fact might affect my judgement of the city and need to be taken into consideration. Leaving the airport you dive directly to the heart of Mombai as the riksha makes its way through the chaotic traffic. We made our way to Salvation Army hotel (following the failling guidance of Lonely Planet) quite close to the western beach. On the way, among the densly buit buildings you can spot some temples and some nice architecture which is unique to Mombai. Leaving our bags at the earlier mantioned guesthouse (which was by far more expensive than the ones we stayed in later) we went outside to scout the nearby bazzar. Encountering for the first time the famous street sellers that try to lure you to buying their products such as drums, flutes and weird Indian guitars. The bazzar is a long narrow road with shops by it's sides and no destinction on the road for padestrians and cars. A mixure that makes it a little difficult to nevigate your way through the bazzar though it's easy to get used to. To our luck we landed on September 17th which by chance was the same date as the start of Ganesh festival (Ganesh is one of the 33 million Indian gods). During festivals we learned later, Indians selebrate as if being possessed which is a significant contrast to their everyday (relative) serenity. We danced with the locals to the sound of their music circling a statue of Ganesh that was placed in the center of the bazzar (one of many). After dining in a local caffe we continued to explore the city, avoiding on the way beggers and sick people. not an admirable aproach, but a nessecery one given the fact that India is home to millions of such people. We finished the day in our room and the next day went straight away to the airport to catch a flight to Delhi. At the time we wanted to leave Mombai as fast as possible, it was only later that we realized Mombai, with a little experience cab be a nice place to stay in for a couple of days.

  

New Delhi - Just like Mombai, Delhi was merely a stop on our way to the Indian Himalayas and so we slept there for only one night. We checked in in a guest house called Hare Rama, located in the 'Main Bazzar' which was pretty nice and had a nice rooftop resturant. Absent the weight of our travel bags we went to a nearby travel agency to book a bus to Manali the following day (An important tip and a lesson you sooner or later learn is that upon arrival to a new destination it is better to find a place for the big bags while searching for guesthouses or aandering around). With the bus booked to Manali the following day we could clamly start exploring the alleys of the main bazzar. The main bazzar is, for what I gather, the main destination for tourists in Delhi mainly for it's backpacker-friendly gesthouses and wide variety of shops that will leave no shopper unsatisfied. As with most tourist-fluded places, the main bazzar is a place of interest for beggers.If you feel like exploring farther and get a glipse to the more formidable scoiety in Delhi take a riksha to one of the many grand hotels and witness the huge gaps in the socio-economic status of the people in India's capital city.

One thing to keep in mind for the early waker such as me, is that life in India is very easy going - mening that even though businesses close early at night they won't necesserily open up early. As I'm writing this I'm sitting in one of the more modern resturants in Jaisalmer, it's 09:30 in the morning and I'm waiting for the waiter to finish washing his teeth so he can take my order. If you have a long trip in India it's recomended to adjust yourself to their schedules and wake up later in the day.

From delhi wo took a semi-sleeper to Manali (a 17 hour ride).

  

Manali - The more you travel in India the more used you get to long rides (giving the magnitude of this subcontinent). A smart traveler wiould avoid eating alot before such rides and avoid dairy at all if possible. I was not as wise on my first bus ride which was to Manali and throughout the 17 hours of ride I had a terrible stomach ache and needed a visit to the W.C very frequently. Nevertheless, bus rides in India have their charms as well. The longer the ride the wider the landscape you cover, and taking a seat by the window is the best bus-riding tip (though not original) I can give. Aspecialy when ascending to Manali in the Himalayas missing the views would be a pitty. Manali is a hill station located in the middle of Himachal Pradesh. As you enter the terrain of the Himalayas and gain height you start ro grasp the magnitude of this mountain chain called Himalaya. No matter how high mountains around you look, as you reach their pick you are serounded by even higher mountains and so on untill you eventually reach summit of the Himalayas, Mt. Everest which we didn't have the privilage of viewing this trip. Manali is at around 2300 meters high and is devided into two - new and old Manali. New manali is the center where the bus station is located and where most of the local shops are. Old manail is north to the new one and is located on a hill slope and is much more traveler-oriented, filled with multi cuisine caffes and low budget guesthouses.The weather in mid september to october (when we were in the himachal) was pleasent with ocasional showers.
