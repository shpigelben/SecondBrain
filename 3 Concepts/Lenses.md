Lenses are transparent objects which are used to manipulate light rays by bending them using either [[refraction]] or [[Diffraction.md]]

# Refractive Lenses
When light passes from one medium to another, its path changes.

## Focal Length
Focal length can be thought of as a measure of how much the lens bends light. The closer the focal distance to the lens the more light bending it is. Lenses with positive focal length are said to **converge** light, while those with negative focal length are said to **diverge** light. The focal length, $f$, can be calculated by the Lens-maker's equation $$ \frac{1}{f} = (n-1)\left[ \frac{1}{R_{1}}-\frac{1}{R_{2}}+ \frac{(n-1)d}{nR_{1}R_{2}} \right]$$Where $R_{1}$ is the radius of curvature of the first surface through which a light ray enters the lens. $R_{2}$ is the radius of curvature of the surface through which the light leaves the lens. $n$ is the refractive index of the lens material, and $d$ is the thickness of the lens. A thin lens approximation is taken in most cases, where the focal length can be more easily calculated as
$$\frac{1}{f} \approx (n-1)\left[ \frac{1}{R_{1}}-\frac{1}{R_{2}} \right]$$
The signs or the radii of curvature depend on whether the vertex of the lens (where its boundary meets the optical axis) is to the right or to the left of the center of curvature. If its to the left, the radius of curvature is positive, if its to the right of the center of curvature the radius of curvature is negative.

> [!NOTE] Double-Convex Lens
> In the thin lens approximation $R_{2}\to-R_{2}$ thus$$ \frac{1}{f} = (n-1)\left[ \frac{1}{R_{2}}+\frac{1}{R_{2}} \right] $$For a symmetric lens $R_{1}=R_{2}\equiv R$ we simply get$$f = \frac{R}{2(n-1)}$$
