Current density describes **how many** ($N$) particles that carry a certain **property** $(Q)$, flow through a cross-sectional **area** $(A)$ at a certain period of **time** $T$. Dimensionally
$$j_{ q} = \frac{N \cdot Q}{A \cdot T}\to \frac{NQ}{L^{2}T} = \frac{N}{L^{3}Q} \frac{L}{T}\to nqv$$
- Generally the current density, which is proportional to the velocity (or average velocity) or the particles is a **vector** quantity
- The property Q can be either:
	- mass $q=m$
	- charge $q= Ze$
	- heat $q = Q$
