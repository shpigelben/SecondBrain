# Massless Particles
$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{E}}{\partial p} = 
\frac{\partial (\hbar\omega)}{\partial (\hbar k)} = 
\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial k} \equiv v_g$$
$$E^{2}=(m_{0}c^{2})^{2}+(pc)^{2}$$
for matter waves we use the De Broglie relations $E=\hbar \omega$ and $\mathbf{p}=\hbar \mathbf{k}$
$$\hbar \omega = m_{0}c^{2}\sqrt{ 1  + \left( \frac{\hbar k}{m_{0}c} \right)^{2}} \quad \stackrel{\hbar k\ll m_{0}c}{\approx\approx\approx\approx} \quad m_{0}c^{2}\left[ 1+ \frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{\hbar k}{m_{0}c} \right)^{2} \right]= m_{0}c^{2}+ \frac{1}{2m_{0}}\hbar^{2} k^{2} $$
The following applies to waves, whether they are of massless or of massive particles
$$
E=\hbar\omega \quad\quad \mathbf{p}=\hbar \mathbf{k}
$$
The energy momentum relationship is what differs between the two

$$
\begin{align}
E_{\text{massless}} &= pc = \hbar c |k|  \\
E_{\text{massive}}  &= \frac{p^{2}}{2m} = \frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}

\end{align}
$$

for ${p\ll mc}$ (massive particle approximation)
$$
\begin{align}
E = mc^{2}\sqrt{ 1+ \left( \frac{p}{mc} \right)^{2}} \longrightarrow mc^{2}+\frac{p^{2}}{2m}
\end{align}
$$
for $p\gg mc$ (massless particle or ultra relativistic approximation)
$$
E = pc\sqrt{ \left( \frac{mc}{p}  \right)^{2}+1} \longrightarrow \frac{mc^{2}}{2}+pc
$$
for waves (matter waves and photons) $p=\hbar k$ and $E=\hbar\omega$
