≤The tale of six pilgrims on their journey to the time tombs on the planet Hyperion. What the time tombs are, along with the labyrinth and the menacing Shrike? We don't know. The exposition, also is light on the details and Simmons establishes his world building through the six short backstories of the pilgrims which constitute the bulk of the novel. Little by little we learn about how humanity destroyed "old Earth" and began colonizing other planets and start systems. We are faced with the concrete limitations of FTL in the form of a time debt which is a seemingly pseudo-relativistic effect. While very soft in terms of scientific accuracy,  it manages to appeal to my mind as a physically feasible constraint. Later we learn that what is referred to as "the Web" are planets connected by what is known as *farcasters* which serve as an immediate (for lack of a better term) bridge between worlds. How is this technology possible we do not yet learn, but connecting a planet to the Web via these devices appears to be neither trivial (not all known\habited planets have it) nor too difficult. 

The story begins from the perspective of the consul, who is being summoned to participate in a pilgrimage to the Shrike temple in the time tombs of Hyperion for some important reason we do not yet know. We do learn of an imminent threat from the Ousters (a human collective, divorced from the Hegemony centuries ago and have been living mainly in empty regions of space and have evolved to be something quite different), who plan to attack or take control of the planet of Hyperion. We learn that the consul has been chosen by the *cult of the Shrike* to participate in this pilgrimage, that it won't be his first time on the planet, and that he is reluctant to return.

As the consul awaked from the fugue (a travel-induced-comma) he meets 6 other people who will share his pilgrimage in the planet of Hyperion. We learn that all have been there in some capacity, but none really seem to understand why they have been summoned to this pilgrimage and what it should ultimately serve.
