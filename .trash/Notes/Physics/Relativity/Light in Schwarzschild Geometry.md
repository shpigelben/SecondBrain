### Conservation of Energy Equation

As in the case of massive body in [[Schwarzschild geometry]], there are conserved quantities for the trajectories of massless particles as well

$$ e = - \xi\cdot u \left(1- \frac{2M}{r}\right) \frac{dt}{d\lambda} \tag{1} $$

$$l = \eta\cdot u \Big(r^{2}\sin^{2}{\theta}\Big) \ \frac{d{\varphi}}{d\lambda} \tag{2}$$

but here the derivation differs by the [[Four Vector| four velocity]] normalization condition that is inherently different for lightlike particles.

$${\Large g_{\alpha \beta}} \frac{dx^{\alpha}}{d\lambda}\frac{dx^{\beta}}{d\lambda} \stackrel{!}{=} 0 \tag{3}$$

choosing $\theta=\pi/2$  as the plane of motion and applying equation $(3)$ with the constants of motion we arrive at

$$ e^{2} = \left(\frac{dr}{d\lambda^{2}}\right)^{2}+ \left(\frac{l}{r}\right)\left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right)\tag{4}$$

by defining a new quantity $b\equiv l/e$ one can rewrite $(4)$ as

$$\frac{1}{b^{2}} = \frac{1}{l^{2}}\left(\frac{dr}{d\lambda^{2}}\right)^{2} + W_{\small eff}(r) \tag{5}$$

Where $W_{\small eff}(r) = \frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(1- \frac{2M}{r}\right)$ is the effective potential of a massless particle in the SWS geometry

#### Impact Parameter

To understand that fact that $b$ is the [[impact parameter]] , we look at the case where $r$ is large enough so that $W_{\small eff}$ vanishes. We can do so since, being the ratio of two conserves quantities $b$ is a conserves charge in will remain the same throughout the entire trajectory.

$$b = \left|\frac{l}{e}\right| \approx  \frac{r^{2}\frac{d{\varphi}}{d\lambda}}{\frac{d{t}}{d\lambda}} = r^{2} \frac{d\varphi}{dt} = r^{2}\frac{d{\varphi}}{dr} \frac{dr}{dt}$$

In large enough distances $\varphi\approx \frac{d}{r}$ and $\frac{dr}{dt}\approx -1$ .
