From the definitions in the continuous [[Hamiltonian Operator]]
$$\large \boxed{\begin{align*} \\ \quad
\hat{\mathcal{H}} = \frac{1}{2m}\left(\hat{p}-A(\hat{x})\right)^{2} +V(\hat{x})\quad \\\
\end{align*}} $$
We saw that there are two phases the a particle in the presence of fields can accumulate.

### Dynamic Phase
The $V(\hat{x})$ term in the hamiltonian is the dynamical phase that accumulates over unit time. It 
$$\left[\vec{V}\right] = \frac{\vec{\phi}}{T}  $$
That means when there exists a field $V$ in space, the longer a particle spends time in it the greater the phase it accumulates

### Geometric Phase
The $A(\hat{x})$ term in the hamiltonian is the dynamical phase that accumulates over unit time
$$ \left[\vec{A}\right] = \frac{\vec{\phi}}{L} $$
That means when there exists a field $A$ in space, the farther a particle moves in the field the larger the phase it accumulates
