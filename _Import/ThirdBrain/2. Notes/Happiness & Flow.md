#note

Happiness is hard to define. When trying to outline its nature I'm tempted to say that it is a state of content in which a person is grateful for the people he's surrounded by and the things he has going for him in life. He is satisfied with his accomplishments, but realizes that there is always room for improvement. He is happy with the process he is going through in various areas of his life and wished to improve upon it.

(That definition has two flaws it depends on external factors and it focuses on the future vs the past)

But that definition requires reflecting and delving into the past, and I believe that happiness has more to do with the **now** than it has to do with the **then**. Also, the way I initially tried to define happiness was very situation dependent, meaning it was subject to external circumstances, some if not all are not in ones control. Achievements, the presence of people in your life, income these can all change by the minute and are a lot of time measured against predetermined standards that paint them in different light. The only things a person can truly aspire to control is his himself.

So if happiness has more to do with the **now** and with the **self** how would one go about defining it (to the extent that it can even be defined)?

For starters I'd say that a person, absorbed in the **now** and isn't bothered all day by future and past events is a happier person. In his book 'Flow', Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi correlates overall happiness with frequent and prolonged periods of flow. I agree with that and I think that another piece of the puzzle is being able to attain a prolific state of flow, on a daily basis regardless of external conditions (as much as possible) is also key. That means two things: 

One - the state of flow should not be prompted by novelty (video games, show etc). As much as possible it should be dependent on the simplest and most available resources so that it can be reproduced every time. 

The second thing is being able to achieve flow regardless of external bothers. A fight with the spouse, financial problems, and the safety of a relative are all types of "bothers" that surround us all the time and are great contributors for living in the past or in the future and hinder our ability to experience the present and focus on our task. This requires a very sturdy emotional resilience which is harder to cultivate for certain people that others. 

___

We must also recognize that working a third of our day (in the best of situations), sleeping for another third and dealing with life on the last third leaves very little room for flow inducing activities. This raises philosophical questions about the way we live in modern days, questions which I will not tackle now. Instead, I feel that those who are able to experience long sessions of flow in modern times either managed to find such professions that allow for it or have learned to avoid the distractions and stimuli that are prevalent in this era. Probably those who feel happiness probably managed to figure out these two out. 

___

# Still the **'then'**...

We still think about the past. It is an irreplaceable part of ourselves and our identity. It what makes us who we are and we have to live with it (hopefully in peace). And as humans we cannot ignore the future, our whole society, science and curiosity deal with predicting **what will** and perhaps use it for improving **what is**. A few ideas about how to live with the **then** as an individual 

make window for planning the future, but avoid thinking about it too much outside of it.

While we can learn a lot from being in the present, the greatest teacher remains the past. As much as we'd like consider ourselves creatures of great and unique intellect, we learn not so differently that do monkeys and cats. We learn by conducting experiments and comparing them to our theories. Reality is the ultimate judge. As memory machines, we store valuable conclusions about our processes and interactions. We (hopefully) learn what seems to be right and what seems to be wrong, and only through reflection we can avoid repeating mistakes and conducting ourselves better in the present.
