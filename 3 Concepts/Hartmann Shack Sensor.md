# Introduction

A perfect image forms when all rays leaving an object later converge at a single point. This rarely (if ever) happens, and in general, optical systems used for imaging suffer from optical abberations which distort the resulting image.

Optical abberations come in various forms but are more broadly divided into either chromatic or achromatic types. **Chromatic** abberations result from the dispersive nature of the optical system which cause different wavelengths to refract at different angles creating different focal points for different colors in an image consequently. **Achromatic** abberations are geometrical in nature and occur when a lack of symmetry exists in the system.

==The advantage of considering optical abberations in terms of wavefront  distortion instead of deviation of light rays, is that wavefront distortion is affected by diffraction unlike rays.==
