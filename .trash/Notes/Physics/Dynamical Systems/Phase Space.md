Phase Space is the space of all dynamical variables, in which all possible configurations of the system live. A solution to the system with certain initial conditions traces a trajectory in phase space which has a one-to-one correspondence to a dynamic behavior in the real world. A __phase portrait__ can be drawn by plotting the different trajectories that arise from various initial conditions
![[image of phase portrait.png]]
__Trajectories in phase space cannot self-intersect__. If such a thing were possible, a solution of the system with initial condition at the point of intersection would have two possible futures which is not possible. Therefore trajectories, which at one point, return to a previous point of the past, will necessarily retrace their history again - periodic motion.

# Quantization of Phase Space
Quantization of phase space is necessary when considering systems of small scales in which quantum phenomena become dominant. This is mainly due to the [[Uncertainty Principle|uncertainty relation]] between two conjugate dynamical variables
$$\Delta x\Delta p \geq \frac{\hbar}{2}$$
which limits the highest possible resolution for a grid in phase space. Each unit in this grid in known as a __Plank cell__ 


$\bullet$ two conjugate variables are related to one another via a [[Fourier Transform]]
$\bullet$ a Planck Cell is defined to be $2\pi\hbar$ when talking about one dimensional problem in position and momentum. One can generalize this #question into $i2\pi[x,p]$ for conjugate variables $x$ and $p$. where $p$ generates translations in $x$.

![[Phase space (ExDr).png]]

$$ V_{\small \text{phase space}} = 2\sqrt{2mE}\times L $$

$$  N_{\small \text{planck cells}} = \frac{V_{\small \text{phase space}}}{2\pi} = \frac{\sqrt{2mE}\times L}{\pi} $$

The number of plank cells corresponds to the total number of possible states of the system
