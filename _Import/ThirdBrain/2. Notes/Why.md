#note 

- Create notes with active recall in mind. Avoid mindlessly pouring content from one place to the other - even if there's benefit in the act in itself. When writing, plan for active engagement be it questions at the top of the article or what not.

Having the knowledge, neatly packed in ones own words is good, but not enough. Whenever available, upon revisiting or referencing material, it is best to first test oneself on the knowledge before revealing it.

> [!NOTE] 
> Obsidian is a good place to store forgotten cognitive and mental processes the one has gone through and got eroded with time and disuse.

Many times in our intellectual journey we go through mental processes that might later remain untouched due to irrelevance or what not. Our brain keeps and maintains whatever is useful to it. Still, the feeling that a good cognitive process might go to waste is saddening, and a lot if times we might discover that we need to recover something we once knew well but that has been eroded. Going through the same mental process for recovery of that idea might be a fruitful endeavor as always, but if we had a window into that study session in which that idea was encoded we might gain most of the benefit and save a lot of time.
