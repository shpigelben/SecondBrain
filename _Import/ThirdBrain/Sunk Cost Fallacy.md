# Example
I think the best way to prove it’s a fallacy is via an example:

Imagine that your favorite thing in the world is to play videogames with the boys on Friday night. There’s nothing you like more than that. But this Friday none of your friends can make it so you guys cancelled it.

Now you have nothing to do on Friday night so you decide to buy a ticket for a concert at your local nightclub. Let’s say you spent 30 dollars on this ticket.

But then, unexpectedly, on Friday afternoon all of your friends are suddenly free and they say that videogame night is back on and let you know about it.

Sunk cost fallacy is going to the concert anyway because you already paid for it instead of going to the thing you know you will enjoy more. Instead of only losing once (spending the 30 dollars) you lose twice (spending the 30 dollars and not doing the thing you like doing more). That is sunk cost fallacy.
