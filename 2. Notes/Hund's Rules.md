The are many __terms__ in the energy spectrum of a multi-electron atom (see [[LS Coupling]]). of these, one will form the ground state, while all others are excited states. Calculating the unique ground state of such an atom is a lengthy task, but Hund's Rules allow us to determine it quite easily so long as the atoms are in the LS-coupling regime. The rules are:

- The term with the largest S $\to$ largest multiplicity has the lowest energy
- For a given S, the term with the largest L has the lowest energy
- For J:
	- $J=|L-S|$ has the lowest energy if the corresponding shell is less than half full
	- $J=|L+S|$ has the lowest energy otherwise

# Hund's 1st Rule
The lowest energy state is the one that maximizes the total spin $S=\sum\limits m_{z}$. Since maximum total spin correspond to parallel spins in a configuration (all $\uparrow$ or $\downarrow$) they all must occupy different subshells due to [[Pauli Exclusion Principle|Pauli's exclusion principle]]. This is why when going up in atomic number the spins occupy the subshells individually (in one direction) - "bus seat rule".
