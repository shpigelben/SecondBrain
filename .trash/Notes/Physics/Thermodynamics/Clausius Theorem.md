#TD1 #thermodynamics 

Let us consider a thermodynamic system that is represented by some [[Thermodynamic Parameters]] and whose equilibrium state lives in some state space spanned by the parameters.

Now consider a transformation that is a [[Thermodynamic Processes|closed cycle]] $\mathcal{C}$ in state space. Since the systems returns to the same point in state space its change in internal energy, which is path independent is zero, and according to the [[First Law of Thermodynamics]]
$$0 = \Delta U = Q - W \Longrightarrow  Q =  W$$
Now imagine that $\mathcal{C}$ is divided into $k$ segments such that the $k+1$ segment is the first. Each segment is extracting heat ${Q^{R}}_{i}$ from a reservoir $T_{0}$ and injecting heat $Q_{i}$ into a corresponding sink $T_{i}$ such that $T_{0} > T_{i} \quad\forall i$ 

For each step we have, by the [[Temperature |thermodynamic temperature]]
$$\frac{{Q^{R}}_{i}}{Q_{i}}= \frac{T_{0}}{T_{i}}$$
The total heat extracted from the reservoir is equal to the total work output by the system, and is given by
$$W_{tot} = Q_{tot} = \sum\limits_{i=1}^{k} {Q^{R}}_{i}= T_{0}\sum\limits_{i=1}^k \frac{Q_{i}}{T_{i}}$$
At the end of the cyclic process, if the entire heat equals zero, that means that heat was converted entirely into work as if the process was generated by ideal [[Carnot Engine]]s, violating the [[Second Law of Thermodynamics]]. Therefore the net amount of heat should be less than or equal to zero, where equality implies a reversible process.
$$\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k} \frac{Q_{i}}{T_{i}} \leq 0 \quad\longmapsto\quad \oint\limits_\mathcal{C} \frac{\delta Q}{T} \leq 0 $$
Where the integration is in the limit of $k\longrightarrow\infty$ . In the case of reversible processes an exact differential that can serve as a state function of the system can be defined, the [[Entropy]].
