$$
T_{n} = \sum\limits_{k=1}^{n} k = 1+2+\dots+n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}
$$
$\forall n$ the numbers $n$ and $n+1$ are **coprime**, namely they don't share the same divisors

> [!NOTE] Why $n$ and $n+1$ are coprime
> Assume $\exists k\in\mathbb{N} \ \text{such that} \ n/k\in \mathbb{N}$.
> This means $(n+1)/k = n/k + \frac{1}{k}$.
> Since $1/k \in \mathbb{Q}$ $k$ is not a divisor of $n+1$.
> 

Therefore any triangular number $T_{n}$ has two coprime numbers, each brings its own unique set of divisors. Thus, the problem reduces to performing [[prime factorization]] on $n$ and $n+1$.
