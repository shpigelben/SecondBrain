- (27/11/92) - When discussing cognitive risks of using AI people often counter that by saying that technology have rid us from seemingly unnecessary abilities, doing calculations in our heads (calculator), navigating (GPS), remembering phone numbers (cellphones), etc... and the cost of losing these unnecessary (?) skills was worth paying for the amount of wealth and comfort that these technologies provided. How far does this claim goes though? If we imagine living in the (allegedly) utopian world (dystopian in my opinion) depicted by Elon Musk in which each and every one of out whims is taken care of by autonomous agents, from eating to commuting to simply existing. Is such a world, in which all agency is taken from humans and outsourced to agents a world worth living in? Isn't the struggle something worth having in life? Something that gives meaning? It appears that, now more than ever, humanity is on the course to a hedonic world. Eudemonic world is out of the question.

- (24/07/26) - *"the cost of losing these unnecessary skills was worth paying"* It is good to point out that these skills are not unnecessary in a general sense. Sure they had their use and might still have, but through these technologies they have become unnecessary today.
