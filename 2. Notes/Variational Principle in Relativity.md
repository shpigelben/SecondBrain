$$
\tau = \frac{1}{c} \int \sqrt{-ds^2} = \frac{1}{c} \int \sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}} 
$$

Parameterizing the trajectory on the world-line with parametrization variable $\sigma$ 

$$
\frac{1}{c} \int d\sigma\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^{\mu}}{d\sigma}\frac{dx^{\nu}}{d\sigma}} \equiv \frac{1}{c} \int d\sigma \mathcal{L}
$$

$\tau$ is the action, and $\mathcal{L} \longmapsto\mathcal{L} \left(x^{\mu},\frac{dx^{\mu}}{d\sigma},t \right)$ is the Lagrangian. The Lagrangian's dependence on the coordinates is through [[Metric Tensor|metric]] $g_{\mu\nu}$. Through the [[Stationary Action | principle of stationary action]] we should get the equations of motion from the [[Euler Lagrange Equations]].

$$
\frac{ \partial\mathcal{L} }{ \partial x^{i} } = 
\frac{d}{d\sigma}\left(
\frac{ \partial\mathcal{L} }{ \partial \left(\frac{dx^{i}}{d\sigma}\right) }
\right)
$$

One can also derive the [[Geodesic Equation]] from the variational principle.
