(a) Plot $\phi_{\scriptsize s}(x-ns)$ for $n=1, \ s=2$.
(b) Does this set of function generate a basis? If so to which space?
(c) Is the set orthogonal?

![center](../9.%20Misc/attachments/Pasted%20image%2020240705173728.png)
**(b) + (c)** We can show that the set of functions generate an orthogonal set, and therefore a basis for a certain space of functions. It is trivial to show that for a certain $s\in \mathbb{R}$, and $n\in \mathbb{N}$ the function satisfies
$$\phi_{\scriptsize s}(x-ns) = \begin{cases}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{ s }} & x\in (ns,ns+s) \\
0 &\text{else}
\end{cases}$$
which means that it is constant on intervals of length $s$ and vanished outside. It is also possible to see that two functions with $n\neq m$ are non-zero in different, non overlapping intervals $[ns,ns+s]$ and $[ms,ms+s]$. With this knowledge it is simple to show the following

$$\small
\begin{align}
\braket{ \phi_{\scriptsize s}(x-ns) | \phi_{\scriptsize s}(x-ms) } &= \int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}  \phi_{\scriptsize s}(x-ns)\phi_{\scriptsize s}(x-ms)\, dx  \\
&=\int\limits_{ns}^{ns+s}  \phi_{\scriptsize s}(x-ns)\cancelto{0}{ \phi_{\scriptsize s}(x-ms) }\, dx  \\
&+\int\limits_{ms}^{ms+s}  \cancelto{0}{ \phi_{\scriptsize s}(x-ns) }\phi_{\scriptsize s}(x-ms)\, dx =\frac{1}{s}\delta_{\scriptsize nm}
\end{align}
$$

Where we assumed, without loss of generality, that $m>n$. This shows that the set is orthogonal (but not orthonormal) and is a basis for the space of piecewise constant functions on the intervals $[ns,ns+s]$. An example of such a function generated by the basis is given below
$$
f(x)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{10} C_{\scriptsize n}\cdot\phi_{\scriptsize s}(x-ns)
$$
with constant coefficients

> [!Question] Question 3
> (a) Prove the parallel law.
> (b) Verify the Parallel Law for the case of interference of two coherent fields as obtained within an intensity sensor.
> (c) For two coherent fields of similar amplitude, under which condition is the sensed intensity minimal?

**(a)** Let us calculate the following

$$\small
\begin{align}
||f+g||^{2} + ||f-g||^{2} &=  \braket{ f+g | f+g } +\braket{ f-g | f-g } \\ 
& ||f||^{2}+||g||^{2} + \braket{ f | g } + \braket{ g | f } \\ +&||f||^{2} + ||g||^{2} -\braket{ f | g } - \braket{ g | f } = 2(||f||^{2}+||g||^{2})

\end{align}
$$
which can be rearranged to fit the required form
$$
\boxed{\begin{align} \\
 \ \ ||f+g||^{2} = 2||f||^{2} + 2||g||^{2} - ||f-g||^{2} \ \ \\ \\
\end{align}}
$$
**(b)+(c)** Let us use the Parallel law for the case of
$$
f(x,y)=Ae^{i\phi(x,y)}, \quad g(x,y)=Be^{i\varphi(x,y)}
$$
The interference of the two waves at a point $(x,y)$ is given by

$$
\begin{align}
I = ||f+g||^{2} &= 2||f||^{2} + 2||g||^{2} - ||f-g||^{2} \\
&= 2A^{2}+2B^{2} - ||Ae^{i\phi}+Be^{i\varphi}|| \\
&=A^{2}+B^{2} + AB(e^{i\delta}+e^{-i\delta}) \\
&=A^{2}+B^{2}+2AB\cos(\delta)
\end{align}
$$
Where $\delta=\phi-\varphi$. For the case where $A=B\equiv A$ we have

$$
I = 2A^{2}\Big[1+\cos(\phi-\varphi)\Big]
$$
And it is clear that the sensor measures minimal interference intensity when
$$
\cos(\phi-\varphi)=-1 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \phi-\varphi = \pi(1+2m)
$$
Which is simply a destructive interference between the fields when their phases are on odd integer multiple of $\pi$ from each other.

> [!QUESTION] Question 5
> Let $T$ be an operator in Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$
> (a) Prove that if $T$ is unitary, its eigenvalues have modulus 1.
> (b) Prove that if $T$ is hermitian, its eigenvalues are real.

**(a)** Lets assume that $v$ is and eigenvector of $T$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$. On the one hand, due to unitarity
$$
\braket{ v | T^{\dagger}T|v }=\braket{ v | I|v } =\braket{ v,Iv  } =\braket{ v,v  }= ||v||^{2}
$$
on the other 
$$
\braket{ v | T^{\dagger}T|v } = \braket{ (Tv)^{*},Tv  }=\braket{ \lambda^{*}v ,\lambda v }=|\lambda|^{2}\braket{ v,v }=|\lambda|^{2}||v||^{2}   
$$
equating the two sides, we can divide by the norm of the vector to arrive at
$$
|\lambda|^{2}=1
$$
**(b)** Let $T$ be now a hermitian operator with similar eigenvector and eigenvalue as let us consider the two following expression. On the one hand
$$
(Tv)^{*} = (\lambda v)^{*} = \lambda^{*}v
$$
and on the other hand
$$
(Tv)^{*} = vT^{\dagger} = \lambda v
$$
equating the two expressions results in 

$$\lambda^{*}=\lambda$$the eigenvalues of $T$ are real numbers.
