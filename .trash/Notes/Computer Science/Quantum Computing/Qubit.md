#quantum #computation #information

A qubit (or quantum-bit) is the most basic unit of quantum information. It is a [[Two State System]] with states.

$$ \ket{0} \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \quad\quad\quad \ket{1} \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} $$

where the two values of a classical bits are simulated for example by the presence or absence of electrical current, quantum bits are manifested in quantum systems with two energy levels differed by $\Delta E =\hbar \omega$ . 

The ground state - $\ket{0}$ - is made by cooling the system to extremely low temperatures $k_{B}T\leq\hbar\omega$


1. unlike a classical [[Bit]] that has one definite value, a qubit can be in a superposition of the two states $\ket{0}$ and $\ket{1}$.
$$\ket{\psi} = \alpha \ket{0} + \beta \ket{1} \quad{\quad{ \alpha,\beta\in \mathbb{C}}}$$

___
1. It can be in a [[Superposition]] of both $0$ and $1$.  If a quantum computer has $n$ qubits, it can be in a superposition of _all_ states ranging from $\ket{0,0,...,0}$ to $\ket{1,1,...,1}$, unlike classical computer that is in only one of the $2^n$ possible states at a time.
2. qubits can be [[Entanglement |entangled]], in contrast with classical [[Bit]]s that are independent of each other.
___

The qubit, being quantum in nature, can generate a phase. It is unmeasureable
