Typical interatomic distances in solids are $\lambda \propto 10^{-8} cm$. An [[electromagnetic wave]] has to have wavelength of that order in order to be able to probe the atomic structure of a solid. This means a corresponding energy of 
$$ \hbar\omega = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \approx \  12.3\times10^3 \ eV \longrightarrow O(KeV) $$
EM radiation with energies of KeV orders of magnitude belong in the x-ray part of the spectrum
![[Light Spectrum.png]]

When light scatters off of rigid periodic solids, it can reveal the internal structure of the ions within. There are two viewpoints for performing this task

1. [[Von Laue Diffraction]] - reciprocal lattice
2. [[Bragg Diffraction]] - direct lattice
