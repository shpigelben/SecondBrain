The acceleration of chemical reactions by illuminated metallic nano-structures is a well-documented phenomenon, yet the underlying mechanism remains contested. Whether this enhancement arises from non-equilibrium “hot” electrons generated by photo-excitation or from localized photothermal heating remains an open question. In this work, we propose a theoretical framework in support of an experimental setup aimed at investigating this distinction. The design employs a thin film of indium tin oxide (ITO) embedded in a thermally isolated desalination tank, enabling controlled measurements of photothermal response. To guide the setup’s design, we characterize the optical properties of existing ITO samples using Fresnel equations in conjunction with the Drude model, allowing for the prediction of the material’s absorption landscape as a function of wavelength and incidence angle. This framework provides a foundation for constructing a more effective platform to isolate and study light-induced thermal effects, serving as a precursor to experiments involving active chemical systems.
