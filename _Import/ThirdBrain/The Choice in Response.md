In response to an assault, a country can choose to respond in broadly three different manners:

1. Target militants and civilians without distinction.
2. Target militants while disregarding collateral damage.
3. Target militants while trying to minimize collateral damage.

The first is clearly an act of war crime, bordering on terrorism, depending on the nature of the response. The second, while immoral, places the responsibility on the governing body to clearly distinguish between civilians and militia and take measures to protect its civilians. The third is the most moral way to conduct an armed conflict between two states.

# In the Context of October 7th
On October 7th, an armed group entered Israel and killed both soldiers and civilians indiscriminately. They slaughtered many more civilians (children, women, and men) than soldiers. This clearly corresponds to the first of the three aforementioned options. Moreover, the methods used to kill civilians and soldiers, and the horrific acts committed during this excursion, **qualify them as terrorists of a devilish kind**. Many would claim that October 7th did not happen in a vacuum and that the ongoing Israeli blockade warrants some sort of excuse, which is utterly absurd.
