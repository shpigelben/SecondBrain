# Configuration Field
Magnetic field due to a single circular loop. Closed-form solution due to Biot-Savart law.
$$
B^{\ell}(\mathbf{r},t) = I(t)\cdot\Big[g_{\rho}^{\ell}(\mathbf{r}) \ \hat{\rho} + g_{z}^{\ell}(\mathbf{r}) \ \hat{z}\Big]
$$
Where each loop's geometry (radius and height) determine the geometric factors $g_{r}$ and $g_{z}$, and $\mathbf{r}=\rho \hat{\rho}+z \hat{z}$ The total field of a configuration, consisting of several circular loops is given by the superposition of the singular fields

$$
\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r},t) = \mu_{0}I(t) \begin{bmatrix}
\sum\limits_{\ell}g_{\rho}^{\ell}(\mathbf{r}) \ \hat{\rho} \\
\sum\limits_{\ell}g_{z}^{\ell}(\mathbf{r}) \ \hat{z}
\end{bmatrix} = I(t) \begin{bmatrix}
g_{\rho}(\mathbf{r}) \ \hat{\rho} \\
g_{z}(\mathbf{r}) \ \hat{z} 
\end{bmatrix} = \mu_{0}I(t)\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{r})\tag{1}
$$

such that having several loops with different parameters boils down to summing their geometric factors for every component.

# Eddy Currents
A magnetic flux through an area induces eddy currents. Ohms law provides the current density. $J_{z}$ and $J_{\rho}$ (need to show?) are negligible in comparison and are therefore not considered.

> [!NOTE]- Skin Depth
> Magnetic diffusion time is $\tau_{d}\approx \mu_{0}\sigma d^2\approx 4 \ [\mu s]$ for $d=42 \ \text{[mm]}$  and $\sigma \approx 1.9\times 10^7 \ \text{[S/m]}$. The rise time of the pulse is $\tau_{p} \approx 10 \ [\mu s] \gg \tau_{d}$ which means the magnetic field has time to fully penetrate the disc such that we are in the quasi-static limit.

$$
J_{\phi}(\mathbf{r},t) = \sigma E_{\phi}(\mathbf{r},t)  \cdot \Pi(\mathbf{r},t) \tag{2}
$$
$\Pi(\mathbf{r},t)$ simply contains the dimensions of the disc, through which currents are able to flow
$$
\Pi(\mathbf{r},t) = \ \Pi_{R_{min}}^{R_{max}}(\rho) \ \Pi_{Z(t)}^{Z(t)+h}(z)
$$
Where $R_{min}$, $R_{max}$ and $h$ are the inner and outer radii and the thickness (static parameters) of the disc, and $Z(t)$ is the $z$ coordinate of the bottom (dynamic parameter) of the disc. This ensures, that at any point in time, the currents are contained within the confines of the membrane.

> [!NOTE] unique definition of this box function
> $$\Pi_{a}^{b}(x)= \begin{cases}x \in [a,b] &\to 1\\ \text{else} &\to 0  \end{cases}$$

The field is calculated from Faraday's law
$$
E_{\phi}(\mathbf{r},t) = -\frac{\dot{\Phi}_{z}}{2\pi \rho} = - \mu_{0} \dot{I}(t)\underbrace{ \frac{1}{\rho}\int\limits_{0}^\rho g_{z}(\rho',z)\rho'\ d\rho' }_{ \equiv G_{z}(\rho,z) } \equiv - \mu_{0}\dot{I}(t)G_{z}(\mathbf{r}) \tag{3}
$$

Inserted back into equation $(2)$ results in a formula for the main eddy currents generated

$$
J_{\phi}(\mathbf{\rho},t) =  - \sigma \mu_{0} \dot{I}(t) \ G_{z}(\mathbf{r}) \ \Pi(\mathbf{r},t)  \tag{4}
$$
# Force Distribution
The force density, generated by the coil's magnetic field on the disc, is the Lorentz force per unit volume, namely

$$
\begin{align}
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{r},t) &= \mathbf{J}\times \mathbf{B} \\
&= J_{\phi}\Big[B_{\rho}(\hat{\phi}\times \hat{\rho}) + B_{z}(\hat{\phi}\times \hat{z}) \Big] \\
&=J_{\phi}\Big[ B_{\rho}(-\hat{z}) +B_{z}(\hat{\rho}) \Big]
\end{align}
$$

> [!NOTE]- Force Ratio
> $$\frac{f_{\rho}}{f_{z}} = \frac{B_{\rho}}{B_{z}} = \frac{g_{\rho}}{g_{z}}$$ and the force ratio is$$\frac{F_{\rho}}{F_{z}} = \frac{\int g_{\rho} \ dV}{\int g_{z} \ dV}$$
> - [ ] Need to find how to insert a $\phi$ dependent bias that accounts for the asymmetry of the coils

We are interested in the force distribution in the z direction as it generates the desired pressure pulse

$$
f_{z}(\mathbf{r},t) = -J_{\phi}(\mathbf{r},t)\cdot B_{\rho}(\mathbf{r},t) \tag{5}
$$
Plugging into $(5)$ equations $(4)$ and $(1)$ respectively yields

$$
f_{z}(\mathbf{r},t) = \frac{\sigma}{2}  \frac{d}{dt}(\mu_{0}I)^{2} \ \underbrace{ G_{z}(\mathbf{r})g_{\rho}(\mathbf{r}) }_{\equiv \mathcal{G}(\mathbf{r})  }\ \Pi(\mathbf{r},t) \tag{6}
$$
This is true for any configuration of loops with similar current and a disc that are all axially-symmetric (respect the cylindrical symmetry).

Since we expect no contraction of the membrane in the $z$ direction, it would be more instructive to consider the radial force distribution on the membrane, as it can definitely bend if subjected to uneven forces.

$$
\mathcal{F}_{z}(\rho,t) = \int \limits_{\infty}^\infty f_{z}(\mathbf{r},t)dz = \frac{\mu_{0}^2\sigma}{2}\frac{d I^2}{dt} \left[ \int \limits_{Z(t)}^{Z(t)+h}\ \mathcal{G}(\mathbf{r})dz \right] \cdot\Pi_{R_{min}}^{R_{max}}(\rho) \tag{7}
$$

==ideally we would like to create a force distribution as even as possible in order for the EM energy to go directly to the translation motion of the entire disc (piston mode) rather than have it excite other flexion modes.==


> [!NOTE] Notes
> - The magnitude of the force and its temporal qualities are  $\propto I \dot{I}$
> - The spatial qualities of the force, unsurprisingly, are solely due to geometric factors $\mathbf{g}$
