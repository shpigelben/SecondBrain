#### Lagrangian in Relativity

The lagrangian in relativity is defined through the [[Metric Tensor| metric]]. We arrive at it from the action which in the case of a spacetime description is the [[Proper Time]] of a particle. 

$$\begin{align*}
\tau &= \int d\tau \\
&= \int \sqrt{-ds^{2}} \\
&= \int \sqrt{-g_{\mu \nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}}\\
&= \int d\sigma \sqrt{-g_{\mu \nu} \frac{dx^\mu}{d\sigma}\frac{dx^\nu}{d\sigma} }\\
&\equiv \int d\sigma \mathcal{L}


\end{align*}$$

The equations of motion, which in the absence of external forces, should result in [[Geodesic Equation]] trajectories are calculated through the [[Euler Lagrange Equations]].

$$\large \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial x^{\mu}}= \frac{d}{d\sigma}\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \left(\frac{dx^\mu}{d\sigma}\right)} $$
