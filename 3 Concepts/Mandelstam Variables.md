The Mandelstam variables are quantities that represent conservation of energy, momentum and angles in a scattering process
$$\begin{align*}
s & = (p_{1}+p_{2})^{2} = (p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}\\
t & = (p_{1}-p_{3})^{2} = (p_{4}-p_{2})^{2}\\
u & = (p_{1}-p_{4})^{2} = (p_{3}-p_{2})^{2}
\end{align*}$$
where $p_1$ & $p_{2}$ are the [[Four Vector|four-momenta]] of the incoming particles and $p_3$ & $p_{4}$ are the ones for the outgoing particles. The Mandelstam variables represent different momenta for three different [[Virtual Particles & The Propagator#The Propagator|virtual particles]] that define three scattering processes

![center](../4%20Misc/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020240603191000.png)

The propagator depends on the conserved four-momentum of the virtual particle $q$ whose nature determines the type of the process

___
# S-Channel (Annihilation)
For S channel process, the conserved four-momentum of the exchanged virtual particle is given by the following
$$q^{2} = (p_{1}+p_{2})^{2} = (p_{3}+p_{4})^{2} = s$$
It is time-like, namely $q^{2}>0$, meaning that its time-like component is bigger than the sum of the squares of spacelike momenta

___
# t-Channel (Scattering)
For t-channel scattering, the conserved four-momentum of the exchanged virtual particle is given by the following
$$q^{2} = (p_{1}-p_{3})^{2} = (p_{4}-p_{2})^{2} = t$$
It is space-like, namely $q^{2}<0$, meaning that its time-like component is smaller than the sum of the squares of spacelike momenta
