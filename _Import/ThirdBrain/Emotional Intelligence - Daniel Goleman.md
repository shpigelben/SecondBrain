The premise of the book is that emotional intelligence (EI) is not less important than the common IQ and perhaps even more so. Emotional intelligence is, in broad strokes, the ability of an individual to recognize his own emotions and those of others and act accordingly in a manner that serves the situation best.

- Our neural circuitry has developed to work best in scenarios ubiquitous to the last 50,000 years, not the last 500.
# Part I - The Emotional Brain
## 1. What are Emotions For?

### 1.1 What are Emotions?

- With inspiration from [the pleasure trap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jX2btaDOBK8&ab_channel=TEDxTalks). Emotions stem from the genetic information of a species, they form in response to environmental queues. These emotions lead the organism to act in a manner that is hypothesized to best serve the propagation of its genes. Whether it be the need to copulate or fight another male for the privilege of doing so, or run away from a predator to save its life.

- The emotional brain has developed in response to stresses present in nature for tens of thousand of years. Environmental stressors which have been changing rapidly in the modern world for the last couple hundreds of years, progress at a velocity much greater than that in which the brain adapts which might explain our inability to think rationally when faces with a rational situation (fight or flight response in the face of an utterly benign presentation).

### 1.2 Two Minds (Fast & Slow?)

### 1.3 How the Brain Grew

- Limbic System - first structure to give rise to what we consider as emotions.
- The "slow", rational brain is a newer structure, developed on top of the "fast", emotional one. From this point of view it is obvious that emotional regulation is at the root of rational behavior.

## 2. Anatomy of an Emotional Hijacking

An impulsive, emotional outburst, that occurs when a center in the limbic brain (as evidence suggests) proclaims an emergency and recruits the rest of the brain to its urgent agenda.

- Not all limbic hijackings are distressing:
	- A hilarious joke can provoke uncontrollable laughter which is also the product of the limbic brain.
	- Intense moments of joy and sorrow can also trigger uncontrollable emotional responses.

### 2.1 The Seat of All Passion (The Amygdala)

The amygdala acts as a storehouse of emotional memory, and thus of significance itself; life without the amygdala is one devoid of personal meanings.

The workings of the amygdala and its interplay with the neocortex are at the heart of emotional intelligence.
