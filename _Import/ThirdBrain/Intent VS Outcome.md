# Proportionality
In light of the war in Israel since October the 7th there have been a lot of discussions about the proportionality of response. We've heard it in the context of the Israeli retaliation to the massacre, in the context of the Iranian missile launches in response to the attack on their facilities in Syria and killing of Ismail Hannia, and in the context of the Israeli response to the ongoing missile attacks from Hezbulla.

I would like to focus on the two latter cases and discuss how it would be best to define a proportional response to missile attack on the sovereign territory of ones country.

I find that in many of the cases, people tend to consider a proportionate response based on the outcome of the provocation rather than to base it on the intent. Let me solidify this notion by considering an example.

Israel has a robust and multi-layered anti-missile system that has taken years and ample funding to develop (and is still developing). The goal of such system is defense of civilians, civilian-facilities and military assets. Nevertheless, its purpose is unmistakably defense. When Hezbulla manages to cause a single casualty on the Israeli side after firing approximately 600 rockets, a naive (and a very human) tendency would be to claim that a proportionate response would be such that would also cost Hezbulla a single casualty. This is an outcome based response and it is a very dangerous one. It is a very dangerous one because setting it as a standard devalues defensive measures such as the Iron done or the Arrow systems which cost a lot of money as opposed to rockets which can be made very lethal with a greater cost efficiency. 

Were Israel to adhere to such concept of proportionality it would soon stop supporting and developing these defense systems and redirect those efforts into creating offensive measures. Every Hezbulla rocket would be answered by an Israeli one (in the absence of interceptions). A situation such as this could have two outcomes one bad the other worse:

- Fewer rockets would be launched in total, but the number of casualties would nevertheless increase due to the lack of defensive measures.
- More rockets would be launched in total and the number of casualties would be even larger.

To value outcome-based response is to devalue defensive precautions which save lives.
To value intent-based response is to devalue aggression and the conduction of warfare from within civilian population.
