In the context of distributing the intensity of training over the week I wonder what would be the most effective distribution for the purpose of health and longevity in general, and give the constraints of modern daily lives.

My intuition (which might very well be flawed) says that in general, a good spread of intensity over the day is better than having a large sedentary portion of the day contrasted by a condensed burst of intense exertion.

![](4.%20Misc/images/Pasted%20image%2020241203093832.png)

Consider this qualitative example of three different types of daily exertions. The graph portrays three different intensities (power in this case = energy/time) all have similar area under (which is total energy expenditure - exertion).

The black line gives the typical exertion of the modern active man - sedentary throughout the day (desk job) and going to the GYM or a jog once. This is one end of the spectrum, the other could be thought of as the other end of the spectrum (given by the red line) which would be a person who has an active physical occupation which has him fairly active throughout the day, but he doesn't do any other physical activity (exercise) outside of that.

# The Question
The big question is - =="Which end of the spectrum is better, and does there exist an ideal distribution given that our goal is health and longevity?"==.

another question - perhaps even more primitive - would be =="Is there an ideal value of exertion for a given person with a given goal?"==.

# My thoughts
I suspect the the answer for the first question tends toward the red end of the spectrum since it best resembles the way in which we evolved as a species.
