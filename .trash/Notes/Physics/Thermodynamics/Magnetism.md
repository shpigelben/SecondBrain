# Magnetic Systems
The magnetic moment of a material is associated with the number of uncoupled electrons in its outer shell. The more uncoupled the stronger the magnetic moment. We consider materials for which the atoms are bounded (not free electrons in metals) so the the magnetic moments of the spins can be considered as sitting on a lattice

__Ferromagnet__ - has nonzero magnetization in the absence of external magnetic field
__Paramagnet__ - has nonzero magnetization only in the presence of external magnetic field

## Magnetization
A vector field that expresses the density of [[Magnetic Dipole|magnetic moments]] in a magnetic material
$$M=\sum\limits_{i}\sigma_{i} \longrightarrow\iiint \sigma dV$$
The differential energy contribution of an element of a magnetic material is
$$dU_{H} = -\mathbf{B}\cdot \mathbf{dM}$$
The intensive external magnetic field and the extensive magnetization are conjugate variables. When compared to mechanical system we can use the following analogy
$$\mathbf{B}\to P ~~~~~~~~~~ \mathbf{M}\to V$$

## Magnetic Susceptibility
Magnetic Susceptibility is a [[Response Function|response function]] of a magnetic system that describes its change of magnetization with respect to a change in an external magnetic field. 
$$\chi_{_{ T}} = \left( \frac{\partial M}{\partial B} \right)_{T}$$
__In paramagnetism__ the systems responds to an external field as disordered dipoles begin to align with the direction of the field, and so the susceptibility is always positive $\chi_{_{ T}}>0$. 
__In ferromagnetism__ the dipoles are already aligned, and so the susceptibility is positive, but ever so slightly $\chi_{_{ T}}>0$
__In Diamagnetism__ the external magnetic field induces an opposite magnetic field within the material in the opposite direction $\chi_{_{ T}}<0$
