Maximal kinetic energy transfer from impactor to target occurs when
$$
m_{\text{disc}}^{\text{opt}} \stackrel{!}{=} m_{\text{eff}}
$$
The effective mass of the target is defined by the density of the target times the effective volume $V_{\text{eff}}$ compressed during the collision.

$$\begin{align}
m_{\text{eff}}&=\rho_{\text{target}}\cdot V_{\text{eff}} \\
&=\rho_{\text{target}}\cdot A\cdot d_{\text{eff}} \\
&=\rho_{\text{target}}\cdot(\pi R_{\text{disc}}^2)\cdot(\tau_{c}*c_{\text{target}})
\end{align}$$
The effective volume is the cross-sectional area of contact times the effective contact depth, $d_{\text{eff}}$ which is determined by the contact time $\tau_{c}$ times the speed of sound in the target medium. In the collision, waves are generated in both media and travel away from the area of collision. The time it takes either of these waves to reflect and return to the area of contact roughly defines the contact time. In this case, it is determined by the disc

$$
\tau_{c}\approx \frac{2h_{\text{disc}}}{c_{{\text{disc}}}}
$$
Putting this all together results in

$$
m_{\text{eff}} = 2\rho_{\text{target}}\cdot V_{\text{disc}}\cdot\left( \frac{c_{\text{target}}}{c_{\text{disc}}} \right)
$$
