# Thermodynamics

#thermodynamics #phenomenology
___
Thermodynamics deals with the macroscopic description of systems, and draws its laws phenomenologically, as opposed to [[Statistical Mechanics]] which is the formulation of thermodynamic ideas from microscopic structures of the system. Both describe systems that are in __thermodynamic equilibrium__. 

When external conditions change, a system may appear to be out of equilibrium, only to settle back to equilibrium after some __relaxation time__.

[[Kinetic Theory]] provides a microscopic description of the transition between equilibria.

a __macroscopic__ system consists of a large number of particles, such that its micro-structures are weakly affected by outside influence. Due to the large number of dynamical elements that occupy a macroscopic system it is highly chaotic, and any attempt to deterministically describe each and every element in it (though theoretically possible) is moot.

The macroscopic system have three well defined quantities : 
- Linear Momentum - of the center of mass of the system
- Angular Momentum - internal angular momentum + that of the center of mass
- Energy - internal energy (U) and center of mass energy

_We mostly work in the COM frame of reference_ so the only quantities of interest are the _internal_ ones, and so a system in **TD equilibrium** can be described by:

- a set of **[[Thermodynamic Parameters | thermodynamics parameters]]** that depend on one another.

$$ \left\{P_{(V,T)},V_{(P,T)},T_{(P,V)}\right\} $$
- They span a **state space** in which all possible configurations of the system are found.

![[State Space.png | 300]]
- The dependence of the TD parameters arises from the constraint of equilibrium which is described by an **[[State Function | equation of state]]**  characteristic to every system.
- Geometrically the state equation can be represented by a manifold in state space, on which all equilibria states exist and all **reversible processes** between equilibria take place.
