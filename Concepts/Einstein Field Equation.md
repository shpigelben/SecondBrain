$$ \begin{pmatrix}
\text{Spacetime} \\
\text{curvature}
\end{pmatrix} {\large\Longleftrightarrow} \begin{pmatrix}
\text{Sources of} \\
\text{mattrer \& energy}
\end{pmatrix}$$
On the one side there is the [[Einstein Tensor]] that contains information about the spacetime curvature, one the other side there is the [[Stress Energy Tensor]] That contains information about matter and energy flux
$$ G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} Rg_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^{4}}T_{\mu\nu}$$
___
# In Vacuum
With the absence of matter $T_{\mu\nu}=0$, and so the right side vanishes
$$R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} Rg_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=0$$
We can also ignore the cosmological term since it is negligible in [[Newtonian Cosmology|non-cosmological scales]], so we have the following
$$\begin{align*}
& R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} Rg_{\mu\nu} = 0\\
\Rightarrow \ & R_{\mu\nu}g^{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} Rg_{\mu\nu}g^{\mu\nu} = 0\\
\Rightarrow \ & R\left(1- \frac{\tau}{2}\right)=0
\end{align*}$$
It happens that the trace of the metric $\tau = g_{\mu\nu}g^{\mu\nu}$ is usually different than 2.  For the equality to hold the [[Ricci Scalar]] must be equal to zero. We're left with the following as the Einstein field equation in vacuum
$$ R_{\mu \nu}=0 $$
