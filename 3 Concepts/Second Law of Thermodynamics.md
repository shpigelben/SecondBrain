The second law establishes the concept of [[Entropy]] as a physical property of a thermodynamic system.

> [!NOTE] Entropy & Internal Energy
> - In an [[Thermodynamic System|isolated system]] with __constant internal energy__ [[Principle of Maximum Entropy |entropy is maximal]] at equilibrium
> - In an isolated system with __constant entropy__ [[Principle of Minimum Energy|energy is minimal]] at equilibrium

# Direction of Heat Flow
Two subsystems, not in mutual [[Thermodynamic Equilibrium|thermodynamic equilibrium]] comprise a large system that is not in internal thermodynamic equilibrium. If in thermal contact the systems will [[Thermodynamic Processes|spontaneously exchange heat]] $2 \to 1$ such that
$$\begin{align*}
dU_{1}=+\delta Q \\  dU_{2}=-\delta Q
\end{align*}$$
The total change in [[Entropy]] (due to additivity) is the net entropy change of the subsystems. Together with the second law of thermodynamics (for spontaneous irreversible processes), we have
$$0<dS = dS_{1} + dS_{2} = \frac{\delta Q}{T_{1}} - \frac{\delta Q}{T_{2}}$$
The above leads directly to the fact that $\large T_{2}\gt T_{1}$. We thus conclude that heat flows from the system of higher temperature to the other one. If no temperature gradient exists, no heat exchange will occur.
