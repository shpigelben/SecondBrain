> *To do the work and let it go,
> for letting letting it go is what makes it stay* Ursula K Leguin

The line between hoarding and collecting meaningful ideas and insights is thin. I tend to hoard. I find it important since my head is filled with ideas, and a lack of outlet risks losing things. On the other hand I don’t tend to process this fleeting information and end up filling up the stack without ever having done anything with it. So while the collection aspect is crucial no less crucial is distillation, and for that a system has to be set up.

Instead of fleeting notes directly:
- fleeting ideas as bullets  in daily note
instead of tasks
- [ ] TODO checkboxes freely in the note

simplification is key. At the end of the day whatever is deemed store-worthy can be turned to a fleeting note of an atomic concept given it has been chewed on enough

- [ ] challenges
- Accelerated perception of time with aging
- A system for reviewing notes
	- fleeting
		- if a fleeting thought or interest has been snoozed for x days - throw it
		- a fleeting thought that has been reviewed should either be promoted to a concept or incorporated into and existing one.
	- concepts
		- review here should be spaced-repetition-based
		- together with elements of active recall
		- ideally a note will be modified in some way
			- addition of new insights
			- improvement of existing text
			- establishing new connections (unforced)
			- improving format and frontmatter
