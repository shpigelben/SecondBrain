The Laplacian is a differential operator gained by taking the [[divergence]] of a [[gradient]] of a scalar function (or the components of a vector-valued function) and it therefore maps a function into a scalar (or a vector in the vector Laplacian case)
$$ \nabla^{2}\bullet = \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\nabla}\bullet = \text{Div}\Big(\text{Grad}(\bullet)\Big)  $$
$${\large\nabla^{2}}: {\Large f}\big(\mathbb{R}^{N}\big)\longrightarrow {\large\mathbb{R}}$$

# Electromagnetism
The electric field is known to be minus the gradient of a potential
$$\mathbf{E} = -\nabla\phi\tag{1}$$
Next, using [[Gauss Law|Gauss shell]] around the charge distribution while substituting (1) 
$$4\pi\rho=\nabla\cdot \mathbf{E} = \nabla\cdot(-\nabla\phi) \tag{2}$$
We get the Poisson equation (Laplace equation for the special case of $\rho=0$)
$$\large\boxed{ \ \nabla^{2}\phi = -4\pi\rho \ }$$
Where the divergence of the gradient is also known as the
$$\nabla^{2}(\phi_{h}+\phi_{\rho})=-4\pi\rho$$
$$\begin{align*}
\nabla^2\phi_{h}&=0\\
\nabla^{2}\phi_{\rho}&=-4\pi\rho
\end{align*}$$
