It's my fifth day in the Andamans now. Right now I'm sitting in a resturant in Havelock island. We had one night in Port Blair (no ferries after 14:00), and took the governmential ferry the day after to this tiny vacation island. Everybody seems to be coming here - backpackers, couples, people on their honeymoon, diving enthusiasts. High season and tourist season officialy starts at December, but November is mostly comfortable weather-wise and costs are much cheaper. The feeling that first strikes you upon arrival is  a sense of solitude. Never have I felt so far from everything. Andaman is an archipeleg which consists of 537 that are 1300 km away from main land in the middle of the Hindu-Pacific ocean.Port Blair is quite noisey and has active lifestyle aswell as buildings and sensation of civilization. Havelock, on the other hand, wrapped with countless palm trees and trees as high as buildings feel like a tourist-populated jungle with an easy acess to the shore.

Weather here seems to have it's own mood and can rarely be predicted. One morning I woke up to see what seems to be an awe-looking storm on it's way to our direction. As the storm made progress toward us and winds began to howl ominously, I was sure I'm about to witness the harshest storm I've encounted thus far, but luckiely mother nature felt sorry for this tiny island and it's inhabitats, and so the storm changed it's direction to wreck another island instead and weather has been geat thoughout that day. Tides are also much more significant than those we have in Israel. The same morning I saw the storm in the distance I noticed that very shallow water persisted for almost a kilometer from the beach! Later I've learned it was on low tide and in the same evening I went for a run on that shore but there was no shore to run on as the tides were high that time. The other night I walked the distance in the shallow water far from the shore and the lights of the island to look and the evening skies. Enchanted by the amount of stars my eyes could capture I didn't notice that in 5 minutes water were knee high and that I have to get fast to shore for in 10 minutes this point will be 5 meters below sea level.

My friend and I were staying in 'Coconut Grove' which is a backpacker - friendly guest house with prices ranging from 400rp-800rp. They also have friendly staff and great food and are highly recommended. Nevertheless, it is possible to find a place for every kind of vacation. From cheap budget to luxury resorts.
