Straight paths have zero acceleration in constant velocities, whereas curved trajectories introduce acceleration.

In curved space, acceleration vectors have no component tangential to the surface, they are purely normal. A trajectory whose acceleration vector has only normal components is called a [[Geodesic Equation]].

To find Geodesics we seek a curve whose tangential acceleration is zero. Most generally one can write the acceleration along a certain path as composed of normal and tangential components

$$ \frac{d^{2}\mathbf{R}}{d\lambda^{2}} = 
\frac{d^{2}R_{n}}{d\lambda^{2}} +
\cancelto{!}{\frac{d^{2}R_{\tau}}{d\lambda^{2}}}
$$

To represent a curved 2D surface ,for example, one can take a flat space $(u,v)$ and apply a certain function $f:\mathbb{R^{2}\longrightarrow}\mathbb{R^{3}}$ such that

$$f(u,v) = \begin{pmatrix}  f_{1}{\small (u,v)}  \\ f_{2}{\small (u,v)}  \\ f_{3}{\small (u,v)}\end{pmatrix}$$

Where the output is still a 2D surface embedded in a 3D space.
